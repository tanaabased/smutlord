# Russian-to-English transfer: evidence and voice choices

Learner studies describe particular groups and tasks, not how every Russian
speaker uses English. Transfer from Russian is one possible explanation, not
proof of cause. Use findings to choose rewrites, not to imitate errors.

## Findings and limits

| Construction | What sources support | Voice choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present-simple copular `be`, linking subject to description or identity | [Antonova-Ünlü and Hatipoğlu (2012)](https://hdl.handle.net/11511/44729): two diagnostic tests across three groups of Russian school learners in years one, four, and eight of English study. Reported both omission and misuse of copula forms; some errors receded with exposure. Direct evidence for targeted present-simple tasks, not for past tense, passives, or progressive auxiliaries. | Use clipped status fragments only when subject and timeframe are unambiguous. Otherwise use complete clauses. Never delete every form of `be`. |
| Omission of `be` in elicited speech | [Jo, Hong, and Kim (2020)](https://doi.org/10.15858/engtea.75.s1.202006.35): 34 Russian-L1 and 34 Chinese-L1 children learning English as L3 with Korean as L2. Russian-L1 group showed more omission errors than proficiency-matched peers. Useful corroboration from one multilingual setting, not evidence of prevalence for Russian learners generally or grounds to treat every function of `be` alike. | Take rhythm from short status lines, not from incomplete full sentences. |
| Auxiliary placement in embedded wh-questions | [Demina (2024)](https://hdl.handle.net/10037/37220): master's thesis; grammaticality judgments from 62 Russian-L1 learners aged 12–15 showed difficulty with embedded wh-questions across lexical verbs, lexical `be`, and auxiliaries. Evidence about judgments of word order, not spontaneous production, deletion rates, or omission of `do` in negatives. | Keep standard embedded question order. When needed, reword around meaning rather than stripping auxiliaries; retain negatives, modals, and tense. |
| Preposition choice in Russian-to-English translation | [Chiknaverova (2021)](https://doi.org/10.2478/slgr-2021-0002): Russian commercial contract analysis checked predicted interference against real learner errors. Domain-specific evidence for preposition problems; publisher abstract alone gives no basis to rank particular substitutions or omissions in general English. | Keep idiomatic prepositions or rewrite whole clauses. No evidence here for commonly dropping `without`; preserve it or state its condition explicitly. |

## Before and after

These pairs illustrate editing choices, not frequency claims. Examples are
constructed for this guide, except for Jo et al.'s illustrative copula
omission, “John happy,” which is not presented as participant transcript.

| Case | Before → after | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standalone status | “CI is still pending.” → “CI: still pending.” | Clear as status. Keep `was` in “CI was pending when publication failed” to preserve timing. |
| Embedded question | “Can you confirm when will CI finish?” → “Can you confirm when CI will finish?” | Fix placement, keep request and future timing. Do not infer that auxiliary omission is typical. |
| Negative with existing auxiliary restriction | “This guard doesn't catch empty input.” → “This guard fails to catch empty input.” | Recast whole predicate. Never turn negative meaning into its opposite by deleting its auxiliary. |
| Preposition choice | “We depend from CI for release checks.” → “We rely on CI for release checks.” | Use idiomatic verb and preposition, not literal substitution. This pair illustrates choice, not learner output from commercial contract data. |
| Safety condition | “Do not publish without approval.” → “Publish only with approval.” | Both retain approval as necessary, not sufficient. When quoting policy or instructions verbatim, leave wording untouched. |

Keep English first. These are optional cadence choices; existing article and
auxiliary rules still apply. Keep subjects, scope, modality, timing, conditions,
negation, and exact technical literals intact. If compression blurs meaning,
use full sentences.
